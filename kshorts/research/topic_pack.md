# Topic pack: Korean childhood nostalgia, cohort born 2000-2005

Prepared for the scriptwriter and fact-checker. No scripts here.

## READ FIRST: how this pack was sourced, and its limits

- WebFetch could not reach any Korean source in this environment (ko.wikipedia.org, namu.wiki, gamemeca.com, m.seoul.co.kr, www.mt.co.kr all failed DNS resolution; those hosts are outside the session's allowed network domains). So NO page was opened in full. Every sourced fact below comes from WebSearch result summaries (title + URL + a short machine-written summary of the page). Treat all of it as "seen in a search summary, not read in the original".
- Because of that, the confidence labels are deliberately strict:
  - **[2 SRC]** = two different URLs in search results support it (still only via summaries).
  - **[1 SRC]** = one URL supports it via a search summary.
  - **[UNCERTAIN]** = sources conflict, or the summary was ambiguous.
  - **[UNVERIFIED-MEMORY]** = my own background knowledge, no source found. The fact-checker must verify before any script uses it. I have kept these to a minimum and flagged them every time.
- Nothing is marked "confirmed" in the sense of "read the primary page". The fact-checker should open the URLs.
- Cohort evidence: for most topics the "evidence this cohort experienced it" is an inference from dates (born 2000-2005 means elementary school about 2007-2017) plus the source saying it was popular among elementary-school children. Where I could not even find a source saying elementary-school children used it, I say so.
- 나무위키 (namu.wiki) could not be reached; thewiki.kr (a namu mirror) appeared in a few search results and is only used as a lead, never as the sole support.

Korean terms: Hangul with romanisation on first use.

## 1. 또봇 (Ttobot) - "Tobot", the car-to-robot transformers

**Category:** Toys / TV. **Popular:** about 2010-2016 (toy launched 2009, anime 2010, 16 seasons by 2016). **Cohort fit: strong.** Cohort were 5-10 in 2010 (older cohort members were in lower elementary school at the peak). Caveat: the target audience skews slightly young (about ages 4-9), so the 2000-2002-born part of the cohort aged out around 2010-2012; the 2003-2005 births are the bullseye.

**Facts (script-usable):**
1. Maker: 영실업 (Young Toys), Korean toy company. [2 SRC]
2. Toy line launched 2009 (one source); about 6.13 million units sold up to the 2014 article's date. [1 SRC for the number; year conflicts, see below]
3. Cars from Kia (기아차): the robots are Kia models, e.g. 또봇X (Soul), 또봇Y (Forte), 또봇Z (Sportage R), 또봇W (Ray), 또봇C (K3), 14 individual robot lines. [1 SRC; the model-to-letter mapping is from one summary, check it]
4. Young Toys' revenue rose from 20.8 billion won (2009) to 76.1 billion won (the year before the 2014/2015 article, i.e. probably 2013 or 2014), and Tobot made up about 60 percent of revenue. [1 SRC; which year "last year" means is unclear]
5. Anime first aired in 2010 and ran through season 16 by the time of one article (sources disagree on the exact month: March 2010 vs 12 April 2010; one article says Kia and Young Toys "presented" the anime in November 2010). [UNCERTAIN on month]
6. The transformation: a car folds into a robot by hand (kids did it manually, no batteries needed). [UNVERIFIED-MEMORY, though retailer pages describe "transforming robot" and a "2-step transformation" for the mini line]
7. Prices today on retailer pages: Tobot Z about 57,000-67,600 won listed, Tobot Y about 28,800-34,500 won, mini X about 7,800 won (these are current/reseller prices, NOT 2010 prices). [1 SRC, current prices only]

**The catch / where it went:** Succeeded by newer lines (Young Toys kept releasing Tobot seasons through the mid-2010s; the toy-car-brand tie-up became the template for other character marketing, per the 2013 Money Today headline "또봇 대박..기업 캐릭터 마케팅 봇물"). Rival 손오공 (Sonokong) pushed 헬로카봇 (Hello Carbot) around 2014 (that item came from the newstomato headline "영실업 vs 손오공, 방학 특수 겨냥 2라운드"; content not confirmed). The 2010 launch-era price and the exact fade date are NOT sourced. [UNCERTAIN]

**Physical actions (3-5):**
- Hold a toy car, fold and click it into robot form, one limb at a time.
- Roll it along a desk, then do the transform and raise it overhead.
- Line up three toys (X, Y, Z) on a shelf and combine them into one big robot (the combined TRITAN set exists per one eBay-listing summary).
- Pull a price tag off the box while looking shocked at the number.
**Key props:** generic car-robot toys in 3 colours (no Kia logos, draw as a generic hatchback), a toy box, a desk.

**Hook idea:** "Your dad's car turned into a robot in your hands, and the car company was paying for it."

## 2. 쫀디기 (jjondigi, also 쫀드기) - the chewy "rubbery" stationery-shop snack

**Category:** Snacks. **Popular:** 1980s-2000s in front of schools, still on shelves in the cohort's time. **Cohort fit: good, with a caveat.** This is NOT a new-to-2000s item; it is an 80s-90s snack that kept selling. Evidence it reached this cohort: a 2013 Seoul Shinmun piece names 쫀디기 among 문방구 과자 hit by the 2013 crackdown, i.e. the same period the cohort was in school. The caveat must be stated: it is a "revival of the older thing" situation, and a script should not claim it was exclusive to the cohort.

**Facts:**
1. Sold at stationery shops for 100-200 won ("네모 스낵" type, a thin long rectangle, flavours: sweet bulgogi and spicy). [1 SRC]
2. Eaten plain, but it was commonly toasted over a flame to puff and soften it. [1 SRC; the Wikitree piece says grilling over direct heat gives "불맛" and chewiness; the 연탄불 (briquette) detail in my query was not confirmed]
3. Stationery-shop snacks in general: in the early 2000s a typical school-front snack was 100 won, per a Gwangju elementary-school-front shop story. [1 SRC]
4. 2013: the government designated "불량식품" (junk/"bad" food) one of the "4대악" (four major social evils) and the food agency was upgraded into the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety in March 2013. [1 SRC for the upgrade, MFDS webzine PDF]
5. A Seoul Shinmun article on 27 April 2013 is headlined "쫀디기·꿀맛나 '우리가 4대악이라고?'… '문방구 과자' 눈물의 폐업" ("We're one of the four evils?" stationery-shop snacks close in tears). The body (seen only as a summary) describes a small Seoul maker going out of business; the company is anonymised as "A사". [1 SRC; DO NOT say the 쫀디기 maker closed, the summary does not confirm that]
6. Oh My News, 27 March 2013: stationery-shop owners protested that supermarket bundles are fine but single items in their shops get branded as junk. [1 SRC, headline-level]
7. Comeback: roasted/"불쫀드기" and "쫀드기 철판볶음" sold in places like Gyeongju's 황리단길 and as bar snacks. [1 SRC, Wikitree]
8. Price now: about 490 won each on 11st (current). [1 SRC]

**The catch:** 2013 crackdown plus the decline of stationery shops; later reborn as a retro/MZ-trend item.

**Physical actions:**
- Pull a stick of 쫀디기 out of a bag, tug it to show it stretches.
- Hold it over a small flame (gas burner prop; keep it clearly a low-poly cartoon flame) and watch it curl.
- Pay with a single 100-won coin on a counter.
- Chew it theatrically, jaw strain.
**Props:** brown strip snack, tongs, 100-won coin, shop counter.

**Hook idea:** "The snack that was allegedly one of Korea's four great evils."

**Sources:**

- https://www.wikitree.co.kr/articles/1060098 (revival; summary)
- https://m.seoul.co.kr/news/2013/04/27/20130427014002 (2013 closing story; could not open)
- https://m.ohmynews.com/NWS_Web/Mobile/at_pg.aspx?CNTN_CD=A0001848340 (shop owners, 2013)

## 3. 달고나 / 뽑기 (dalgona, ppopgi) - the sugar-and-soda candy you carve

**Category:** Snacks / stationery-shop. **Popular:** decades; the 50-won cart/stationery version was a 1970s-90s thing, still on street corners in the cohort's time. **Cohort fit: medium.** Born-2000s kids met it as a leftover tradition and, much later, via the 2021 Netflix show (outside their childhood). Don't claim it was a 2008-2014 fad.

**Facts:**
1. Sugar melted in a ladle (국자) over a flame, baking soda (식소다) added, stirred with a chopstick until it puffs. [2 SRC: Kyunghyang 2021 piece and thewiki.kr summaries]
2. The puffed sugar is pressed flat, a shape (star, cross etc.) is stamped, and if you extract it without breaking it you win a free second one, hence the name 뽑기 ("pulling"). [1 SRC]
3. Regional names: 국자, 쪽자, 뽑기, 또뽑기, 띠기. [1 SRC, mirror wiki, treat as lead]
4. A price of 50 won appears only in a personal recollection post on 10000recipe.com; do not state it as the general price. [UNCERTAIN]
5. Chemistry: baking soda releases carbon dioxide when heated, which puffs the sugar. [1 SRC]

**The catch / where it went:** Street and school-front sellers declined with the stationery-shop collapse and food-safety enforcement (see topic 2). Cohort relevance rests on this being a leftover tradition.

**Physical actions:** Stir a ladle over a burner; sprinkle soda and watch it foam; press a flat disc; stamp a star; carefully trace around the star with a needle.
**Props:** metal ladle, burner, disc press, star stamp, needle.

**Hook idea:** "Lick the star to cheat" (the lick-the-back trick is [UNVERIFIED-MEMORY]).

**Sources:** https://khan.co.kr/article/202109302017005 , https://thewiki.kr/w/달고나 , https://www.10000recipe.com/talk/125495

## 4. 문방구 오락기 and 뽑기 기계 (stationery-shop arcade machines and capsule machines)

**Category:** Stationery-shop. **Popular:** peak in the 1990s-early 2000s; the law changed 2007-2008. **Cohort fit: weak-medium.** The cohort were 2-7 in 2007; the oldest members may remember the tail. The strong version of this topic is "the machines that vanished right before you were old enough". Say so explicitly.

**Facts:**
1. Stationery-shop recollections: capsule toy machines where you turned the lever until the toy you wanted came out, and coin machines where you played rock-paper-scissors for medals. [1 SRC, a Brunch/Insight-type recollection summary]
2. YTN reports that "사행성 오락기" (gambling-like game machines) targeted children and police cracked down. [1 SRC, YTN piece, 2023 retrospective]
3. 2007: the National Assembly education committee passed an amendment to the School Health Act that would ban game machines around schools, including in stationery shops and supermarkets, starting the second half of the following year. [1 SRC; only the "passed committee" stage is confirmed]
4. 2006: the "바다이야기" (Sea Story) gambling scandal triggered regulation of arcades. [1 SRC, blog-level]
5. Smelly "방귀탄" (fart bombs) and rubber eyeball toys were sold, then banned for safety reasons. [1 SRC, YTN]

**Physical actions:** Insert a coin, crank a lever, open a capsule, play RPS with a machine, get punished by teacher-figure silhouette (keep generic).
**Props:** capsule machine, coins, medals.

**Sources:** https://www.ytn.co.kr/_ln/0103_202306030630440915 , https://www.hangyo.com/mobile/article.html?no=22343 , https://weekly.khan.co.kr/article/14538 , https://thewiki.kr/w/문구점

5. Informal tournaments existed in fan communities, but nothing was found about 문방구 (stationery-shop) tournaments. [UNCERTAIN; I did not find the "stationery-shop arena" detail]

**Catch:** the cohort's wave faded by about 2012-13 (UNVERIFIED-MEMORY, no source).
**Physical actions:** Load a ripcord launcher, rip it, drop the top into a stadium dish, narrate the clash, one flies out.
**Props:** launcher, stadium bowl, tops.

## 17. 우유 급식 (school milk)

**Category:** School habits. **Cohort fit: good**, thinly sourced.

**Facts:**
1. School milk was introduced in national elementary schools in 1970 and later extended to middle and high schools. [1 SRC]
2. A 2007 paper puts the participation rate at about 49 percent overall, centred on elementary schools. [1 SRC]
3. In a survey of 518 Seoul elementary pupils, 88.2 percent took school milk but only 47.9 percent were satisfied; stomach trouble and taste were the main reasons for drinking less. [1 SRC]
4. Pupils wanted flavoured milk and yoghurt; teachers saw strawberry and vanilla preference as the reason for wasted white milk (5 or 6 cartons thrown out daily per class at one Seoul school). [1 SRC]
5. A Kyunghyang Weekly piece (March 2018) is titled "획일적 우유급식, 꼭 해야 하나?". [1 SRC, headline-level]

**Physical actions:** carry a milk crate, punch a straw through foil, drink the white milk, covertly trade for strawberry.
**Sources:** https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO201107654735899.page?lang=ko , https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO200736038173827.pdf , https://weekly.khan.co.kr/article/201803051740431

## Ranked top 5 for a first episode

Criteria: forgotten-memory hit for this cohort, act-out-ability with low-poly props, fact solidity, low risk.

1. **또봇 (Tobot).** Aligned exactly with 2010-2015, extremely easy to act out (transform a toy), a clean "catch" (the car-company tie-up and the rival toy wars), and low risk because the car logos can be drawn generic. Facts are decent but need the fact-checker to re-confirm the year and sales figure.
2. **쫀디기 (jjondigi).** Fits the "stationery shop" visual perfectly (flame, coin, counter), and the 2013 "four great evils" crackdown story is a ready-made catch with dates. Must be framed as an old snack that the cohort also ate, not as an exclusive cohort item.
3. **마법천자문 (Magic Hanja Scrolls).** Very strong cohort hit, highly physical (shouting hanja spells with gestures), and the numbers (about 20 million copies) are checkable. Needs the 2002 vs 2003 start year resolved.
4. **컬러링 (ringback tones).** An "I had completely forgotten that" moment shared by every kid who called a parent's phone, with a firm 2002 launch date and a 2020 V컬러링 coda. Weaker on visual drama and on kids' own experience.
5. **닌텐도 DS.** Strong cohort object, clamshell and stylus are easy to build as a low-poly prop, and the Korean launch dates (2005-12-29, 2007-01) are sourced. Held back by the missing Korean price and the odd 1 million claim.
